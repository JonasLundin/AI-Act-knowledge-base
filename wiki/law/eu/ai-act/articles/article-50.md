# Summary

**Article 50 (Transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems)** establishes horizontal notification and transparency duties designed to protect citizens from deceptive, undisclosed, or synthetic AI content[^regulation-eu-2024-1689].

# Application Schedule & Transitional Reprieve
- **General Application Date**: **2 August 2026** (24 months following entry into force pursuant to Article 113).
- **Transitional Reprieve under Article 111(4)**: Inserted by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744[^regulation-eu-2026-1744], systems placed on the market or put into service before 2 August 2026 are granted a transitional reprieve until **2 December 2026** to implement synthetic content watermarking and disclosure mechanisms.

# The Four Transparency Pillars

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|               ARTICLE 50 TRANSPARENCY MANDATE MATRIX              |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. AI-HUMAN INTERACTION (Article 50(1))                           |
|    - Providers must ensure AI systems interacting with natural    |
|      persons inform them that they are interacting with an AI     |
|      (e.g. conversational agents, chatbots), unless obvious from  |
|      the points of view of a reasonable person.                   |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. SYNTHETIC AUDIO, IMAGE, VIDEO, TEXT (Article 50(2))            |
|    - Providers of generative AI systems must ensure outputs are   |
|      marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as        |
|      artificially generated or manipulated.                       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. EMOTION RECOGNITION & BIOMETRICS (Article 50(3))               |
|    - Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization |
|      systems must inform individuals of their exposure.           |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. DEEP FAKES & PUBLIC INTEREST TEXT (Article 50(4))              |
|    - Deployers of deep fakes (manipulated image, audio, or video) |
|      must visibly and prominently disclose artificial origin.     |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

# Machine-Readable Watermarking Standards
Under Article 50(2), technical watermarking solutions must be:
- Effective, interoperable, robust, and reliable as far as technically feasible;
- Proportionate to the state of the art and specific characteristics of the media modality;
- Capable of detection by automated crawlers and platforms.

# Exemptions and Article 50(7)
Under Article 50(5) and 50(7) (as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), transparency obligations do not apply to AI systems authorised by law for law enforcement criminal detection/investigation, nor to content that has undergone human review and editorial control where an editor holds legal responsibility.
